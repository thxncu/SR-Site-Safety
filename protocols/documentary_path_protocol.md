# Documentary paths and implementation checks

## Selection and analytical role

The 13 cases are purposively selected to expose different documentary operations and limits. They include a numbered single-target reference, a distributed reference list, structured associations, an exact-title hypothesis, an ambiguous name, a historical stage mismatch, a change in screening status, two externally reviewed exclusions, an applicability exclusion, a residual possibility raised in discussion, an empty retrieval pool and a below-threshold candidate. The set is illustrative and nonexhaustive. It is not a representative sample from which success rates or the prevalence of a problem can be estimated.

`data/process_tracing_cases.csv` records case-level selection rationales, outcomes and limits. The term process tracing identifies the following of documentary steps; it does not claim causal identification of institutional behavior or a reconstruction of the complete history of each decision. `data/process_tracing_steps.csv` separates source observations, reviewer interpretations, identifier resolutions and analytical inferences. The step order is the order required to inspect the presented path, not a claim that the listed operations were the original decision makers' workflow or that the recorded objects were created in that sequence.

`data/worked_documentary_paths.csv` provides a compact case-level account of the observations, operations, destination or search lead, and inference limit. It points to the numbered step records and, where relevant, to exact relations in `data/provenance_relations.csv`. References to raw FileMaker tables specify field names and physical record identifiers; PFEP identifiers are retained separately because a FileMaker record number is local to its table. Report-based evidence identifies the report, section and printed page. Source URLs identify the official archive or report.

## Reading a path

A documentary path can contain several kinds of relation. An imported inventory description may refer to another PFEP. SKB may retain a process or variable selection in a structured field. A target PFEP may have a relation-specific handling or non-handling statement. An analyst may infer a candidate from its title or taxonomy. Those statements have different authorship and evidential force, even when they coexist in one database.

Each path therefore preserves origin, relation direction, assessment stage, identifier namespace and target state. Existence of a target confirms a resolvable reference or association. It does not, by itself, show that SKB adopted the relation as complete coverage of an excluded record. A broad factor list may supply ambiguous names or include targets that are themselves excluded. A target's relevant status likewise does not imply that every aspect linked to it is modelled. In PT2, all three associations reached through M 1.3.03 contain a reason for non-handling; PT1 contains explicit handled-aspect text at the target's six downstream associations.

In PT13, the imported response refers the preceding interface factors to four local FEPs. The list does not allocate one distinct aspect to each target. Each branch is followed as a separate documentary reference, with the target-specific handling qualification retained.

An earlier-stage identifier is resolved in the stage named by the source field. PT11 consequently resolves ProvBio01 to the provisional SR-Can Quaternary deposits category. A valid historical target is preserved without inventing an SR-Site migration. PT4 compares dated discussion with final status and retained handling relations; it does not treat every retained relation as active after reclassification.

The two review cases preserve reviewer attribution. The SSM 2012:34 reviewers propose Bu20 for K 1.23 and F01 for the record called ESFR22 in their report. The latter is normalized to its canonical database identifier, E SFR-22. Both reviewer criticisms are accompanied by the report's qualification that the processes are captured through other project lists. No reviewer-proposed relation is relabeled an SKB decision.

## Search failures and stop conditions

The two search-stop cases distinguish different operations. N 1.1 has no supplied IFEP membership, so the declared shared-IFEP eligibility rule admits no candidates. N 1.3.4 has eight eligible relevant candidates and a baseline top score of 0.33571428571428574 for I 266, below the stated 0.34 threshold. Its word and character components are zero because all four non-title source fields used by retrieval are empty. Its baseline score therefore reduces to 0.25 times title similarity plus the constant 0.15 shared-IFEP contribution. Reusing the existing title as the empty TF-IDF query, while holding fitted vectorisers fixed, changes the top candidate to M 1.3.04 at 0.6079885485902901. The eligible pool and the underlying documentary evidence are unchanged.

These observations concern the normalized metadata, source-field construction and exploratory retrieval rule. They do not establish a probability of equivalence, a substantive gap in the assessment, or an independent difference in institutional performance between inventories. Changing the gate, source-text construction, weighting or cutoff can change search output without changing any source-documentary relation. The supplied top candidate remains a search lead until its content and provenance are examined; no unrecorded human equivalence judgment is supplied.

## Proposed implementation checks

`data/documentary_implementation_checks.csv` contains six proposed checks tied to the cases. Each row separates the observed condition, proposed record fields, an automatable check, the remaining technical judgment and the inference limit. The `proposed_responsible_role`, `review_trigger` and `followup_record` fields specify a suggested owner, application event and record of disposition. They are normative design proposals rather than observed staffing, completed follow-up actions or evidence of effective implementation. The checks are design implications of the cases, not claims that a particular software system is currently deployed, that the checks were performed by the original decision makers, or that implementing them would prove repository safety.

Checks IC1–IC5 concern documentary representation or its interpretation: relation origin and coverage scope, component-qualified identifiers, temporal disposition, handling versus non-handling, and the scope of applicability reasons. IC6 concerns the secondary retrieval analysis: field availability, eligibility, component scores and threshold margins. The `secondary_analysis_risk` column records how an analyst could misread the source condition, independently of whether the source record itself needs a change.

The paths identify operations and possible points of misinterpretation. They do not measure reader time, cost, task difficulty or reviewer performance. No qualitative confidence label, inter-coder agreement, independent adjudication or unrecorded historical assessment is used to establish substantive equivalence.
